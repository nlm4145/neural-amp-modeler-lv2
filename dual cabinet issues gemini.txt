2. WHY THE "BOOMY FIX" COMMITS DO NOT FIX DUAL-CABINET BOOMINESS
================================================================================

Allowing negative phase alignment (`-10..+10 ms`), polarity inversion
(`B POL INV`), and independent Cab A/B level trims does not fix dual-cabinet
boominess at default settings, and dialing in phase/delay adjustments to fight
boominess typically degrades the tone further.

A. Equal-Power Summing (`0.7071`) Causes an Automatic +3 dB Low-End Boost
   When Dual Cabs Are Active
   ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   Look at the dual-cabinet stereo blend math in `Plugin::processPostChain`
   (src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1320-1326):

     if (haveA && haveB) {
       const float theta = static_cast<float>(kPi) * 0.25f * (1.0f - w);
       const float near = std::cos(theta);
       const float far = std::sin(theta);
       outL = aL * near + bL * far;
       outR = aR * far + bR * near;
     }

   - At default `stereo_width = 0%` (`w = 0`, `theta = pi/4`), both `near` and
     `far` equal `sqrt(2)/2 ~= 0.7071` (-3.01 dB).
   - An equal-power pan/mix law (`-3 dB` per branch, `near^2 + far^2 = 1`) only
     preserves unity loudness for UNCORRELATED signals:
     * In the MIDS and HIGHS (500 Hz - 6 kHz), two different cabinet captures
       have different speaker breakup modes, mic capsule distances, and phase
       dispersion, so their mid/high energy is partially uncorrelated and sums
       close to 0 dB (power sum: `sqrt(0.5 + 0.5) = 1.0`).
     * In the LOW END (60 - 250 Hz)—where the fundamental cabinet resonance
       ("thump"/"boom" around 80-140 Hz) lives—wavelengths are huge (~10 ms at
       100 Hz) and almost all guitar cabinets are minimum-phase and COHERENT
       (in-phase) with one another.
     * When two coherent low-frequency signals are scaled by `0.7071` and
       summed (`0.7071 * A + 0.7071 * B`), they sum in amplitude to `1.4142x` =
       +3.01 dB!
     * Even when `stereo_width` is increased (`w -> 1`), both speakers or
       headphone drivers radiate that coherent 80-200 Hz fundamental into the
       room/listener, producing a persistent ~3 dB low-frequency shelf boost
       relative to the mids/highs the moment Cab B is engaged.

B. Why Phase Alignment (`cab2_delay`) & Polarity (`cab2_polarity`) Cannot Fix
   Dual-Cab Boominess (and Often Make It Worse)
   --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   - Phase alignment (`ALIGN` near 0 ms) maximizes low-end boom:
     Aligning time-of-arrival between two captures brings their low-frequency
     fundamentals into maximum phase coherence, which MAXIMIZES constructive
     low-end summing (+3 dB).
   - Small delays (0.5 - 2.0 ms, including the built-in `LEAD FOCUS` 0.5 ms and
     `WIDE ROOM` 1.2 ms presets in `applyCabConsolePreset:`,
     src/nam_rig_ui.mm:486-493) scoop the mids while keeping the bass boom:
     * A delay `dt` creates its first destructive comb-filter cancellation
       notch at `f = 1 / (2 * dt)`:
       - At `dt = 0.5 ms`, the first notch is at 1000 Hz (scooping midrange
         attack/definition).
       - At `dt = 1.2 ms`, the first notch is at 417 Hz (hollowing out lower-mid
         body).
     * Meanwhile, at 100 Hz (period = 10 ms), a `0.5 - 1.2 ms` delay is only
       18 to 43 degrees of phase shift, so 100 Hz STILL sums almost completely
       constructively (+2.4 to +2.9 dB)! Scooping 400-1000 Hz while leaving
       100 Hz boosted makes the dual-cabinet blend sound even boomier and more
       hollow.
   - Polarity inversion (`B POL INV`):
     With near-zero delay, inverting Cab B cancels the fundamental bass
     completely, leaving a thin, nasal, out-of-phase comb-filtered tone rather
     than a natural cabinet balance.

C. Moving `cab_level` to Cab-A-Only (`ffb705e`) Changed Nothing at Default 0 dB
   ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   - At default `0 dB` trims (`cab_level = 0 dB`, `cab2_level = 0 dB`), moving
     `cab_level` before the sum is a `1.0x` unity multiplier either way—the
     audio output at default settings is bit-identical to before.
   - Furthermore, if you previously pulled `cab_level` down (e.g. -3 dB) to
     tame dual-cab boominess when it scaled the post-blend sum, turning down
     `cab_level` now only attenuates Cab A while leaving Cab B at full volume.

D. Shared Pre-Cab Speaker Impedance Resonance (`speaker_thump` / `speaker_resonance`)
   ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   - When `speaker_profile` is active (or set to `Auto`, which resolves from
     Cab A's path in `src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:933-934`), `SpeakerDynamics`
     imparts nonlinear low-frequency excursion (`speaker_thump`) and resonant
     impedance peaks (`speaker_resonance`) to the shared post-amp signal BEFORE
     it splits to Cab A and Cab B (`src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1013-1016`). Both
     cabinets then reinforce that same pre-boosted low-end resonance in-phase.

3. CHANGES & INTERACTIONS THAT CRITICALLY HURT SOUND QUALITY
================================================================================

A. Dual-Cab `.nam` Oversampling Mismatch, Extra Filter Cascade, and Integer
   Latency Compensation Error (Cemented in `0ee62bb`)
   ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   - In SINGLE-CAB mode (`parallelCabs == false`), when Cab A is a `.nam`
     model, Pedal + Amp + Cab A run inside ONE shared True-Nx upsample/
     downsample cascade (`processTrueGroup` in
     `src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1064-1093`).
   - As soon as Cab B is enabled (`parallelCabs == true`), line 1065 of
     `src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp` (`const bool deferredCab = st == 2 && (irs[2] !=
     nullptr || parallelCabs);`) forces Cab A out of the Amp's True-Nx domain:
     1. The Amp's True-8x stream is downsampled back to base rate after the Amp.
     2. Cab A (`.nam`) is then upsampled to 8x and downsampled back to 1x a
        SECOND time via `Plugin::processTrueCab`
        (`src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1225-1229`), putting Cab A through 6 extra
        half-band filter passes (3 up + 3 down at 8x).
     3. Meanwhile, Cab B (`.nam`) is hardcoded to `kOsLegacy2` (base rate, no
        True oversampling) in `Plugin::stageOversample`
        (`src/nam_rig_plugin.h:417-420`), `Plugin::scheduleModelLoad`
        (`src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1596-1598`), and `Plugin::work`
        (`src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:337-338`).
     4. To compensate for Cab A's extra `processTrueCab` cascade latency,
        `src/nam_rig_plugin.cpp:1270-1276` delays Cab B by an INTEGER number of
        base-rate frames (`cascadeLatencyFrames(cabFactor)` = 23 / 35 / 41
        frames for 2x / 4x / 8x).
   - WHY THIS HURTS SOUND QUALITY:
     The 3-stage half-band cascade (`23 + 12 + 6` taps across 2x/4x/8x) has a
     non-integer group delay (~40.5 to 41.25 samples) plus frequency-dependent
     phase dispersion near Nyquist, whereas `cascadeLatencyFrames(8)` delays
     Cab B by an integer 41 samples with zero half-band phase shift. Blending
     a `.nam` Cab A (which went through `processTrueCab`) with Cab B (`.wav` or
     `.nam` at base rate) introduces a ~0.5-sample fractional delay mismatch +
     high-frequency phase rotation, causing high-frequency phase cancellation
     (comb-filter roll-off in the top octave) that makes the dual-cab blend
     sound noticeably darker, duller, and boomier.

B. Non-WaveNet (e.g. LSTM) `.nam` Models in True-Nx vs Cab2 Legacy Mode
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------
   - In `deps/NeuralAudio/NeuralAudio/NeuralModel.cpp:92-130`,
     `OversampleNAMConfig` only adapts sample rates for `"architecture":
     "WaveNet"` (`if (arch != "WaveNet") return;`).
   - Any LSTM `.nam` model loaded into a True-Nx stage runs at Nx the sample
     rate without dilation scaling, whereas the same `.nam` model loaded into
     Cab B runs at 1x base rate (`kOsLegacy2`).

C. High-Frequency Loss from Linear Interpolation in `AlignDelay`
   (`e2f8eba` / `src/space_fx.h:35-42`)
   -------------------------------------------------------------
   - At `cab2_delay == 0.0 ms` (with `domainDelayMs == 0`), `AlignDelay` is a
     bit-exact bypass (`src/space_fx.h:73-79`).
   - However, whenever ANY non-zero fractional delay is dialed in on `ALIGN`
     (either positive on Cab B or negative on Cab A via `cabAlign` added in
     `e2f8eba`), `DelayLine::readFrac` (`src/space_fx.h:35-42`) uses 1st-order
     linear interpolation:

       const float a = read(whole);
       const float b = read(whole + 1);
       return a + (b - a) * frac;

   - At a half-sample fractional offset (`frac = 0.5`), linear interpolation is
     a 2-point moving average filter `H(w) = cos(w / 2)`, which attenuates high
     frequencies by up to -3 dB at 12 kHz and -6 dB at 16 kHz (at 48 kHz sample
     rate) on the delayed cabinet—dulling pick attack and top-end air while
     leaving low-frequency boom untouched.

4. RECOMMENDED DSP FIXES FOR DUAL-CABINET BOOMINESS & SOUND QUALITY
================================================================================

1. Compensate Coherent Low-Frequency Summing in Dual-Cabinet Mode:
   - When both `haveA` and `haveB` are active (`parallelCabs`), the `< 200 Hz`
     bass region sums coherently (+3 dB with `0.7071` equal-power weights),
     while `> 250 Hz` sums closer to uncorrelated power (0 dB).
   - Fix: Either apply an automatic ~-3 dB low-shelf trim (around 180-220 Hz)
     whenever both Cab A and Cab B are active (scaled by their relative levels
     so muting one cab restores 0 dB), or use a frequency-dependent crossover/
     pan law that sums `< 180 Hz` with coherent amplitude compensation (`0.5`
     /-6 dB) and `> 250 Hz` with equal-power compensation (`0.7071` / -3 dB).

2. Replace Linear Interpolation in `DelayLine::readFrac` with Allpass or
   3rd-Order Lagrange / Cubic Interpolation (`src/space_fx.h:35-42`):
   - A 4-point (3rd-order) Lagrange or Thiran allpass fractional delay
     preserves flat high-frequency magnitude response across the entire audio
     band when adjusting `ALIGN` (`cab2_delay`).

3. Run Parallel `.nam` Cab A at Base Rate (Matching Cab B) or Keep Cab A in the
   Amp's Oversampled Domain Without a Second Cascade:
   - Avoid downsampling after the Amp only to immediately re-upsample and
     re-downsample Cab A in `processTrueCab` while Cab B runs at base rate.
   - Running both `.nam` cabinets at the same rate domain eliminates the extra
     6 half-band filter passes on Cab A and eliminates the fractional group-
     delay / phase-dispersion mismatch between Cab A and Cab B.
